# LAST ORBIT — PHASE 2A

## Core Experience and Hazard System

Status: creative direction accepted; detailed content remains iterative  
Date: 25 October 2026  
Project relationship: independent side project; no Solar System Explorer code or documents changed

## 1. Design outcome

Last Orbit will not depend on one repeated catastrophe. The game will contain a **Crisis Pool**: several different space hazards drawn from natural astronomy, speculative technology, cosmic life, temporal anomalies, and absurd interplanetary civilization.

For the first playable prototype, all Crisis cards share the same mechanical result:

> When you draw a Crisis, your flagship is disabled unless you use an Emergency Reroute. After surviving, secretly place that Crisis anywhere in the Flight Deck. Your turn ends.

Different identities produce visual variety, story, humour, animation potential, and memorable moments without introducing different probabilities or balance effects too early.

Later, an optional **Unstable Universe** module may give individual Crisis cards unique effects. That module is excluded from Rules v0.1.

## 2. Fictional logic

### The Solar Rally Network

The Grand Solar Rally does not use a fixed racetrack. Every flagship is connected to a shared automated system called the **Solar Rally Network**. It continuously assigns gates, flight corridors, gravity assists, refuelling windows, and legal transit routes.

The Flight Deck represents the future sequence of route conditions generated by that network—not literal time itself.

When a captain encounters a Crisis and uses an Emergency Reroute:

1. the ship survives or bypasses the danger;
2. the captain secretly manipulates the Rally Network;
3. the dangerous route condition is reassigned somewhere else in the course;
4. another ship may encounter it later—or the same player may accidentally meet it again.

This supports any kind of hazard. The player is not physically moving a solar flare or temporal rupture; the player is changing where that danger appears in the shared route.

It also explains:

- private deck inspection as navigation intelligence;
- deck shuffling as network recalculation;
- secret reinsertion as route manipulation;
- attacks as forced route assignments;
- skips as emergency detours;
- cancellation as interference with a network command;
- visible deck size as the remaining length of the rally.

## 4. Initial Crisis Pool

The base creative pool contains twelve concepts. Only the number required for the current match enters the Flight Deck. For example, a four-player match randomly selects three Crises.

### Natural crises

#### C01 — Rogue Comet Convoy

A supposedly solitary comet arrives with an entire illegal convoy of smaller fragments.

- Visual: bright fractured ice bodies crossing the route at impossible angles.
- Comic opportunity: the navigation system insists the comet has right of way.
- Scientific seed: comet nuclei, debris streams, high relative velocity.

#### C02 — Coronal Mass Ejection

An immense solar plasma eruption sweeps through the assigned corridor.

- Visual: a wall of luminous plasma and magnetic arcs.
- Comic opportunity: the ship's safety system offers sunscreen recommendations.
- Scientific seed: solar activity and charged particles.

#### C03 — Asteroid Family Reunion

Thousands of related fragments converge for reasons no astronomer can explain.

- Visual: dense-looking close encounter while background text clarifies the intentionally exaggerated density.
- Comic opportunity: every asteroid wears the same mineral-pattern “family badge.”
- Scientific seed: asteroid families created through collisions.

### Technological crises

#### C04 — Dormant Defense Grid

An ancient automated weapon network wakes up and mistakes the rally for an invasion.

- Visual: dark satellites unfolding around the ship.
- Comic opportunity: its operating system demands a security update before firing.
- Origin remains deliberately unknown for future lore.

#### C05 — Grey-Goo Pit Stop

Repair nanites decide the entire flagship is a damaged component and begin “fixing” it into raw material.

- Visual: metallic fluid crawling over the hull.
- Comic opportunity: the nanites proudly report 137% repair completion.

#### C06 — Planet-Eater Prototype

A small, unfinished world-consuming machine selects a flagship as a calibration snack.

- Visual: absurdly overengineered industrial jaws and warning labels.
- Comic opportunity: manufacturer sticker says “For training purposes only.”

### Space-time crises

#### C07 — Micro-Singularity Snare

An artificial gravitational trap catches the flagship's trajectory.

- Visual: lensing, warped stars, stretched ship reflections.
- Comic opportunity: loose objects orbit the captain during the emergency.

#### C08 — Yesterday Loop

The ship repeatedly arrives one minute before it decided to enter the sector.

- Visual: multiple translucent versions of the flagship arguing with one another.
- Comic opportunity: the crew becomes increasingly annoyed by its previous selves.

#### C09 — Probability Leak

Every possible version of the ship briefly tries to occupy the same location.

- Visual: alternate ship configurations flickering together.
- Comic opportunity: one alternate crew is visibly having a much better day.

### Cosmic-life crises

#### C10 — Void Grazer

A colossal peaceful organism mistakes the flagship's energy wake for food.

- Visual: elegant dark creature visible mainly through bioluminescent edges.
- Comic opportunity: it behaves like an affectionate animal while crushing the ship.

#### C11 — Plasma Leeches

Small solar organisms attach themselves to the engines and drink the exhaust.

- Visual: glowing translucent creatures clustered around propulsion vents.
- Comic opportunity: the crew manual classifies them as “mostly adorable.”

### Civilized absurdity

#### C12 — Interplanetary Impound Order

The Solar Traffic Authority remotely disables the ship over an unresolved parking violation from three generations ago.

- Visual: enormous holographic wheel clamp surrounding the flagship.
- Comic opportunity: appeals form require authorization from the captain's future descendant.

## 5. Crisis selection rules

### Prototype v0.1

- Build a pool of at least six text-only Crisis cards.
- At setup, randomly select a number equal to players minus one.
- All selected Crises have identical mechanical rules.
- Players know which Crisis identities may be present only if the chosen cards are revealed during setup; this needs an A/B test.
- Crisis identity never changes probability or survival requirements.
- Crisis cards cannot be cancelled, stolen, traded, scanned into a hand, or used as ordinary resources.

### Recommended initial six

1. Rogue Comet Convoy.
2. Dormant Defense Grid.
3. Micro-Singularity Snare.
4. Yesterday Loop.
5. Void Grazer.
6. Interplanetary Impound Order.

This selection deliberately represents all five creative categories and demonstrates the intended cool-to-comic range.

## 6. Emergency Reroute

Every player begins with one **Emergency Reroute**.

Proposed card rule:

> Play immediately after drawing a Crisis. Discard this card instead of being disabled. Without viewing or rearranging other cards, secretly return the Crisis anywhere in the Flight Deck. Your turn ends.

### Clarifications

- Emergency Reroute is a response, not a Command.
- It may be played only against a drawn Crisis.
- It cannot be cancelled.
- It does not consume one of the player's two Commands.
- The player does not draw a replacement card.
- The surviving player may place the Crisis on top, on the bottom, or between any two cards.
- Other players may not inspect the insertion point.
- The player may hold the deck below the table in physical play or use a private position selector digitally.
- The Crisis remains the same identity after reinsertion.

## 7. Tone architecture

Recommended tonal ratio:

- **60% impressive/cinematic:** the threat should initially look real and spectacular.
- **30% character comedy:** captains and crews react according to their personalities.
- **10% outright absurdity:** occasional events such as the Impound Order prevent the universe from becoming self-serious.

This produces comedy through contrast. A beautiful cosmic disaster becomes funny because of the crew, bureaucracy, or malfunction—not because everything is visually silly.

## 8. Crisis visual sequence for the future digital game

Every Crisis can use the same readable interaction structure:

1. Draw card and freeze ordinary input.
2. Show the Crisis title and target player.
3. Reveal a short establishing animation.
4. Display the available Emergency Reroute response.
5. If no response exists, disable the flagship with a short faction-specific consequence.
6. If rerouted, show the escape and open a private deck-position control.
7. Return to the public table without revealing the chosen position.

The sequence must be skippable after players have seen it. The game state must remain understandable without animation.

## 9. Prototype rule structure selected

The first Rules v0.1 draft will use:

- five support cards plus one Emergency Reroute per player;
- exact deck recipes for 2, 3, 4, and 5 players;
- players-minus-one randomly selected Crisis cards;
- a maximum of two Commands on a normal turn;
- any number of eligible Countermeasures during reaction windows;
- one mandatory Flight Deck draw unless an effect ends the current flight obligation;
- a provisional maximum Flight Pressure of four owed sectors;
- no faction powers;
- no Reactor Charge;
- no unique Crisis effects;
- no campaign, progression, or equipment upgrades.

## 10. Creative direction decisions

The owner granted broad creative freedom and confirmed that multiple hazards may draw from astronomy and science-fiction traditions. Development therefore proceeds with the recommended defaults below unless later testing or owner feedback supports a change.

### D02 — Cosmic life: DECIDED

- **A. Include strange living space creatures — selected.**
- B. Limit hazards to astronomy and technology.
- C. Keep cosmic creatures for a later expansion.

### D03 — Crisis knowledge at setup: PROVISIONAL DESIGN DECISION

- **A. Reveal the selected Crisis identities, but not their deck positions — selected for v0.1.**
- B. Keep both identity and position secret.
- C. Reveal only the creative categories present.

These decisions remain testable. They are not protected from evidence-based revision.
